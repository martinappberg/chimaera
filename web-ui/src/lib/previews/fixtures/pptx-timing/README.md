# PowerPoint timing corpus

Decks whose animations and transitions were written by PowerPoint itself, and the timeline each one must parse to. Two parsers read them and must agree: the viewer's (`../../pptxTiming.ts`, TypeScript) and the PowerPoint plugin's (`chimaera-plugin-pptx`, Rust, which vendors this folder at a pinned commit). A change to a golden file is a contract change: review it as one, and change both parsers with it.

## What is here

- `decks/*.pptx`: the corpus. Small, synthetic, no real content.
- `goldens/<deck>.json`: what each deck parses to (format below); `goldens/cases/<case>.json`: what each case parses to (one slide, `part` the case's file name, `id` 0). Written by `npm --prefix web-ui run timing:golden` from the TypeScript parser, then checked by a person against PowerPoint's own slide show, once. A rule about what PowerPoint does must cite a file PowerPoint saved (a deck in `decks/`) or a frame of PowerPoint's own exported film; a rule without one is a guess, not a contract (R18's first wording, "paths add up", was such a guess and the film disproved it). After that it changes only through a reviewed diff.
- `cases/*.xml`: hand-written slide parts for what the scripting model can't make and for the rules' edges, written by `make/make_cases.py` in the structure of the corpus decks' own slide XML (and ECMA-376 for the on-shape-click sequence). They are **not yet confirmed against a PowerPoint save**: `interactive-trigger` (an on-shape-click sequence whose shape the main sequence later shows too), `bg-paragraphs` (a filled box built by paragraph: `:bg`, paragraphs, then the whole shape leaves), `repeat-autorev`, `default-trigger`, `empty-group`, `over-nesting`, `transition-plain`, `transition-unknown-choice`, `path-edges`, `hostile-strings`, `xml-semantics` (R23a, R29's prefixed attributes), `colour-caps` (R21's colour caps), `held-nonfinite` (R31), `anim-keys` (R28's keyframes), `paragraph-order` (R31's paragraphs over overlapping ranges, R32's body); and, written by `make/make_contract_cases.py`: `text-whitespace` (R23a's four whitespace characters), `colour-chain-interactive` and `colour-chain-split` (R21's chain cap in each sequence on its own), `colour-xmlns` (R21: namespace declarations are not attributes), `path-behaviour-end` (R18: a behaviour's own end orders paths), `split-any-behaviour` (R32: a visibility set on a paragraph splits), `values-cap` and `values-under-cap` (R21's values cap, either side of 250,000), `values-untargeted` (R21 with R3: a behaviour without its own target counts nothing), `value-elements` (R28 and R29: which elements are values), `path-grammar` (R19's words), `text-markup` (R23a: child markup and an interior CR LF in an element's text).
- `fallbacks.json`: what the viewer plays as a fade (below).
- `colors.json`: colour-modifier vectors (R33), from PowerPoint's film of flat fills (`make/make_colors.py`).
- `make/make_corpus.py`: rebuilds the decks. python-pptx lays out named shapes; PowerPoint for Mac, driven in the background through its scripting model, adds the effects and transitions and saves. Needs a Mac with PowerPoint. Rerunning it changes ids inside the files, so regenerate the goldens with it.

## What PowerPoint does (observed, PowerPoint for Mac 16.115)

- Slide ids (`p:sldId@id`) survive a save. Relationship ids (`rId…`) are renumbered: never keep one.
- A duplicated shape id is repaired on save: the later shape gets a new id. Shape ids are otherwise kept.
- Shapes made in PowerPoint carry `a16:creationId`; shapes a script made do not get one when PowerPoint saves the file.
- The main sequence is `p:timing/p:tnLst/p:par/p:cTn[nodeType=tmRoot]/p:childTnLst/p:seq/p:cTn[nodeType=mainSeq]`. Each child `p:par` whose start condition is `delay="indefinite"` is one click. Inside it, one `p:par` per group that starts together, then one `p:par` per effect, whose `p:cTn` carries `presetClass`, `presetID`, `presetSubtype` and `nodeType` (`clickEffect`, `withEffect`, `afterEffect`).
- A slide that starts by itself has a first click group like any other (`delay="indefinite"`) whose first effect is `withEffect` or `afterEffect`, not `clickEffect`. That group runs on entry.
- Every entrance begins with `p:set style.visibility = visible`, every exit ends with `hidden`. That, not the preset, says what is on screen.
- A text box built by paragraph has one effect for its background (`p:spTgt/p:bg`) only when the box has a fill, then one per paragraph (`p:txEl/p:pRg st end`). Child paragraphs are `withEffect` in their parent's click. `p:bldLst/p:bldP build="p"` records the choice; a chart gets `p:bldGraphic`.
- Effect durations live on the behaviours, not the effect node. A `p:set` is written with `dur="1"`, yet PowerPoint starts the "after previous" effect behind an appear at 0. Transparency writes `dur="indefinite"` (until the slide ends).
- PowerPoint writes an "after previous" effect's start into the delay of the group around it, so the delays along the tree already say when each effect starts.
- A transition is wrapped: `mc:AlternateContent` with `mc:Choice Requires="p14"` (duration in `p14:dur`, ms) or `Requires="p159"` for Morph (`p159:morph option="byObject|byWord|byChar"`), and an `mc:Fallback` (for Morph, a plain fade). `advTm` (ms) is on `p:transition`.
- Presets seen (class, id, subtype): appear `entr 1`; fly `2` with subtype 1 top, 2 right, 4 bottom, 8 left; blinds `3`; box `4`; circle `6`; dissolve `9`; fade `10`; split `16`; wheel `21`; wipe `22` with the same side subtypes; zoom `23` subtype 16; float `42`. Exits use the same ids with class `exit`. Emphasis: fill colour `1`, grow/shrink `6` (`p:animScale by 150000`), spin `8` (`p:animRot by 21600000`), transparency `9`, darken `24`, teeter `32`. Paths: class `path`, `p:animMotion path="M 0 0 L 0.25 0 E"` in fractions of the slide.
- Not matched by the viewer, which turns a shape about its centre: PowerPoint's spin of a shape with text turns it about a point a fixed distance left of the centre. Measured on film edges (1920 px wide, a 13.33 in slide): plain dark rectangles with text, no line and no effects, turned 90°, 180°, 270° and −90°, move their centre by (−5.5, +4.5), (−10, 0), (−4.5, −4.5) and (−5, −5) px, which is one pivot 5 px (about 32000 EMU) left of the centre for every turn (a true pivot offset, not a rasterisation offset); the same for a box twice as wide, twice as tall, or with a 1 pt line; none without text, and none (0, 0) with a 12 pt outline; a shadowed one moves (−0.8, +6.5) after 90°, a picture (−0.3, −0.2). Why text, a thick outline and a shadow change it is not known (films `spin2`, `spin3`, `spin4` of the harness; not in the corpus).

## The effect vocabulary

One name per effect, shared by both parsers, the plugin's timeline text and its writer. Class is part of the name where a pair exists.

| Name | From |
|---|---|
| `appear` / `disappear` | `entr`/`exit` 1 |
| `fade-in` / `fade-out` | 10 |
| `fly-in` / `fly-out` (+ `from`/`to` `top` `right` `bottom` `left`) | 2 |
| `wipe-in` / `wipe-out` (+ side) | 22 |
| `zoom-in` / `zoom-out` | 23 |
| `float-in` / `float-out` | 42 |
| `split-in` / `split-out`, `wheel-in`, `circle-in`, `box-in`, `blinds-in`, `dissolve-in` (and `-out`) | 16, 21, 6, 4, 3, 9 |
| `grow-shrink`, `spin`, `transparency`, `teeter`, `fill-color`, `darken` | `emph` 6, 8, 9, 32, 1, 24 |
| `path` | class `path` (any id), with its path |
| `other` | anything else: kept and shown by PowerPoint's preset numbers, never rewritten |

Fly and wipe carry `dir`, the side from the subtype (`top` `right` `bottom` `left`); every other effect has `dir: null`.

Transitions: `none` (an empty `p:transition`), `fade`, `fade-black` (`p:fade thruBlk="1"`), `push`, `wipe`, `cut`, `morph` (`p159:morph`), `other`; `dir` is the transition element's `dir` as PowerPoint writes it (`l` `r` `u` `d`; absent means its default, `null`).

## Played as a fade

`fallbacks.json` lists the transition filters (`p:animEffect filter`, by the name before any parenthesis) that the viewer does not draw as PowerPoint does, each with the effect names that use it and why. An entrance or exit using one plays as a fade over its duration and lands on its state; every other filter in ECMA-376's grammar (`barn`, `blinds`, `box`, `checkerboard`, `circle`, `diamond`, `dissolve`, `fade`, `plus`, `randombar`, `strips`, `wedge`, `wheel`, `wipe`, with their subtypes) is drawn, `dissolve` and `randombar` through a random pattern of cells or bars, fixed per shape, and the rest as moving regions laid out as PowerPoint's film of the `filters` deck shows them. The plugin reads this list to tell an agent what a deck will not look like in the viewer; `pptxMotion.test.ts` fails when the viewer falls back on a filter not listed or draws one that is. Beyond filters, a behaviour the viewer cannot read (an attribute it does not animate, a value that is not a number or a formula it knows, a part that never ends) makes its entrance or exit fade and its emphasis play nothing; the slide's tooltip names each.

## Golden format

An abridged example (the real `goldens/triggers.json` differs):

```json
{
  "deck": "triggers.pptx",
  "slides": [
    {
      "part": "ppt/slides/slide1.xml",
      "id": 256,
      "transition": { "kind": "fade", "dir": null, "dur": 2000, "advanceAfter": 3000, "advanceOnClick": true, "morph": null },
      "steps": [
        [],
        [
          { "trigger": "click", "start": 0, "dur": 500, "class": "entr", "preset": 10, "subtype": 0,
            "effect": "fade-in", "dir": null, "target": "2", "sets": "visible" },
          { "trigger": "with", "start": 0, "dur": 500, "class": "entr", "preset": 10, "subtype": 0,
            "effect": "fade-in", "dir": null, "target": "3", "sets": "visible" },
          { "trigger": "after", "start": 500, "dur": 500, "class": "entr", "preset": 10, "subtype": 0,
            "effect": "fade-in", "dir": null, "target": "4", "sets": "visible" }
        ]
      ],
      "states": [
        { "hidden": ["2", "3", "4", "5"], "moved": {} },
        { "hidden": ["5"], "moved": {} }
      ],
      "interactive": 0
    }
  ]
}
```

The golden is the JSON of the parser's output (numbers printed as ECMAScript's `Number.prototype.toString` prints them: `2`, not `2.0`; `0.25`; `1e-7`; `1e+21`), pretty-printed with 2 spaces and a final newline; key order is as above (`path` after `sets`, only on `path` effects; `approximate` last, only when set).

- `transition`: `null` when the slide has no `p:transition`; otherwise R13–R15.
- `steps`: R6–R8. `start`, `dur`: R9–R11. `class`, `preset`, `subtype`: `presetClass` as written, `presetID`, `presetSubtype` (R2; 0 when absent). `effect`, `dir`: the vocabulary above. `target`: R3, R4. `sets`: R12 (an effect with a `null` target still reports it, and changes nothing). `path`: the first `p:animMotion` path, as written.
- `states[k]` is the slide after step `k` (`states.length == steps.length`): R16–R19; and what emphasis holds, R24–R31: `scale`, `turn`, `opacity`, `color`, in that order after `moved`, each present only when it has an entry (so a deck without emphasis has goldens with `hidden` and `moved` only).
- `interactive` counts on-shape-click sequences (R20).
- `approximate`: R21, R22.

Regenerate the goldens with `npm --prefix web-ui run timing:golden` (`pptxTiming.test.ts` with `TIMING_GOLDEN=write`; it refuses to run when `CI` is set). `npm --prefix web-ui run test` compares against them, and fails on a golden without a deck or case.

## Rules

Both parsers implement exactly these; the corpus and the cases pin them.

- **R1 Hostile strings.** Every string the file supplies is data, never a name in the implementation: a file-supplied key (`__proto__`, `constructor`, any id) must behave like any other key, with no inherited or built-in entry reachable through it.
- **R2 Integers.** An integer attribute is an optional `-` and 1–9 ASCII digits, nothing else (no whitespace, no `+`, no exponent, no fraction); anything else counts as absent. Absent `presetID`, `presetSubtype` and delay are 0. A negative delay, `dur`, `repeatCount`, `advTm` or `p14:dur` counts as 0.
- **R2a Decimals.** A real number in the file (`p:animScale`'s `x` and `y`, a `fltVal`, a numeric `strVal`) is, with no surrounding whitespace: an optional `+` or `-`, then ASCII digits with an optional `.` and more digits, or a `.` and digits, then an optional `e` or `E`, an optional sign and ASCII digits. Nothing else is a number: no hex, octal or binary forms, no `Infinity` or `NaN`, no Unicode whitespace, and a blank value is absent, not 0 (PowerPoint writes none of these). `p:animRot`'s `by`/`from`/`to` and a `p:tav`'s `tm` are R2 integers.
- **R3 Shape ids.** A `spid` is 1–10 ASCII digits; anything else means the behaviour (or trigger, or build label) has no target. A behaviour without a target changes no state.
- **R4 Target keys.** `"4"` is the shape, `"4:bg"` its background (`p:spTgt/p:bg`), `"4:p1-1"` paragraphs `st`–`end` (`p:txEl/p:pRg`, from 0; a `pRg` without two non-negative integers means no target). A character range, sub-shape or chart element targets its whole shape. An effect's target is the first target among its behaviours, in document order.
- **R5 Combining keys.** A shape is drawn unless its own key is hidden; a paragraph is drawn unless its paragraph key or its shape's key is hidden; `:bg` (the box behind the text) is drawn unless its key or its shape's key is hidden. A set on the whole shape does not change paragraph or `:bg` keys. A visibility set's `to` counts only as R28's value element allows (R12: a PresentationML `p:strVal` of `visible` or `hidden`).
- **R6 Effects and groups.** The main sequence is the `p:seq` under the `tmRoot` whose `p:cTn` has `nodeType="mainSeq"`; each child time container of it is a click group. Inside a group, an effect is a `p:cTn` with `presetClass` or a `nodeType` of `clickEffect`/`withEffect`/`afterEffect`; other time containers are walked through. A click group that yields no effects is skipped: it is not a click.
- **R7 Default trigger.** `clickEffect`, `withEffect`, `afterEffect` are `click`, `with`, `after`. An effect node without one of these is `click` when it is the first effect of its click group, else `with`.
- **R8 Step 0.** After skipping empty groups, the first group is step 0 when the parsed trigger of its first effect is not `click`; otherwise step 0 is empty and that group is click 1. Every slide has step 0: a slide without animation is `"steps": [[]]`.
- **R9 Start.** An effect's `start` is the sum, in ms, of the start delays of every time node from just below its click group down to the effect node itself. A time node's delay is its first `p:stCondLst/p:cond` without `evt` whose `delay` is an integer (R2), else 0. PowerPoint writes an after effect's start into its group's delay, so the corpus test checks that every `after` starts when everything before it in its step has ended.
- **R10 Behaviour end.** A behaviour's end = its delay (summed like R9, from the effect node down) + dur × (repeatCount/1000, default 1) × (2 if `autoRev` is `1`/`true`). A dur that is absent, malformed or `indefinite`, or a `repeatCount` of `indefinite`, makes the end not finite. A `p:set` whose dur is exactly 1 counts as dur 0 (PowerPoint's instant: an appear lasts 0 ms, and the after effect behind it starts at 0).
- **R11 Effect dur.** The span is the latest finite end among the effect's behaviours; if none is finite, `dur` is null. The effect node's own `repeatCount`/`autoRev` then multiply the span the same way. An `indefinite` repeatCount on the effect or on any of its behaviours makes `dur` null. Compute in exact integers (thousandths, then millionths of a ms) and round half away from zero once, at the end.
- **R12 Sets.** A visibility set's value is read by R28's value-element rule: its `p:to`'s first child must be a PresentationML `p:strVal` whose `val` is exactly `visible` or `hidden` (any other element, namespace or text is no value, and the set is not a visibility set here, nor for R16 and R17). Among an effect's `p:set` behaviours on `style.visibility` with value `visible` or `hidden`, the one that ends latest (R10; a non-finite end is latest) decides `sets`; on a tie, the later in document order. None: `null`.
- **R13 Transition choice.** `p:transition` is read from the slide's children, or from inside an `mc:AlternateContent` child: the first `mc:Choice` whose every `Requires` prefix resolves (in that element's scope) to the p14 (`http://schemas.microsoft.com/office/powerpoint/2010/main`) or p159 (`http://schemas.microsoft.com/office/powerpoint/2015/09/main`) namespace, else `mc:Fallback`. `p:timing` is found the same way.
- **R14 Transition kind.** The transition's first child other than `p:sndAc`/`p:extLst` decides: none → `none`; `p:fade` → `fade`, or `fade-black` with `thruBlk` `1`/`true`; `p:push`, `p:wipe`, `p:cut` → their names; `p159:morph` → `morph`, with `morph` its `option` (`byObject` when absent); anything else → `other`. `dir` is that child's `dir` as written, else null; `morph` is null for every other kind.
- **R15 Transition timing.** `dur` is `p14:dur` (an integer, R2) when present, else from `spd`: `fast` 500, `med` 750, `slow` 1000; an absent or unknown `spd` is 500. `advanceAfter` is the integer `advTm`, else null. `advanceOnClick` is false only for `advClick` `0` or `false`.
- **R16 Initially hidden.** Before step 0, hidden = the targets whose first visibility set, reading the main sequence in document order and then the interactive sequences in document order, is `visible`.
- **R17 Applying a step.** A step's visibility sets apply in the order they end (effect `start` + R10 end; a non-finite end goes last), ties in document order: `visible` removes the key from hidden, `hidden` adds it. `hidden` is written sorted in plain string order (keys are ASCII).
- **R18 Moved.** A motion path is an offset from its target's layout place, never from where an earlier path left it: a second path on the same target replaces the first (PowerPoint's own exported film of `paths` slide 2 shows the shape snap back to its layout x as the second path, `M 0 0 L 0 0.25` with `origin="layout"`, begins, and end at `[0, 0.25]`; the `order` deck's slide 3 film shows the same for `Restart`, which ends 0.25 below its layout place, and `Chained`, whose second path `M 0.25 0 L 0.25 0.25` starts where the first ended and ends 0.25 right and 0.25 down, never 0.5 right: at 13.0 s its left edge is 479 px right of its layout place on a 1920 px film). For each `path` effect, each `p:animMotion` with a path sets its target's offset to that path's end point (R19), applied in the order the behaviours end (the behaviour's own end, effect start + R10, not its effect's: as R17; one that never ends goes last; ties in document order), so the path that ends last decides. A path PowerPoint's own UI chains after another is written to start where the first ended (its string begins at that point), and ends where it ends. Round each coordinate to 4 decimals half away from zero (as `sign(v) × round(|v| × 10⁴) / 10⁴` in IEEE doubles), and write 0 for −0. Every target a path has moved so far is in `moved`, even at `[0, 0]`; keys sorted.
- **R19 Path end.** A path string is split into words at XML whitespace (space, tab, line feed, carriage return) and commas, empty words dropped; a word is a command (one ASCII letter) or a number (an R2a decimal, so `.5.5` and `0.5?` are not numbers). The first word that is neither, or a number whose value is not finite (`1e999`), ends the string there: the walk goes on over the words before it. Walk the commands from (0, 0): `M x y` moves and sets the subpath start, and coordinate pairs after it beyond the first are line-tos (they do not reset the subpath start); `L x y` is a line-to; `C x1 y1 x2 y2 x y` ends at `x y`; lowercase `m`/`l`/`c` are relative to the current point; `Z` returns to the subpath start; `E` or the end of the string ends the path; an unknown command, or numbers no command can take, stops the walk, and the point reached so far is the result. `M 0 0 0.1 0.1 Z` → `[0, 0]`. The viewer plays a path by this same walk (`walkPath`: its pieces are lines, cubics and jumps), so the end it plays to is the end the state holds.
- **R20 Interactive sequences.** A `p:seq` under the `tmRoot` whose `p:cTn` has `nodeType="interactiveSeq"`, started by `evt="onClick"` on a `p:spTgt`. It is counted in `interactive`, never put in `steps`; its effects take part only in R16 (so its entrance targets start hidden, and main-sequence effects then show or hide them like any other target).
- **R21 Caps.** The slide part is at most 4 MiB of UTF-8 (checked first, before scanning; the count is of the part's bytes as UTF-8, an unpaired surrogate in a decoded string counting 3 bytes as its U+FFFD replacement would); the `p:timing` source at most 512 KiB of UTF-8; at most 2,000 effects, counting every `p:cTn` in `p:timing` that has `presetClass` or one of the three effect `nodeType`s, interactive sequences included; at most 300 click groups in the main sequence (empty ones included); nesting counts `p:par`/`p:seq`/`p:excl` containers starting at 1 for the root `p:par` under `p:tnLst`, and more than 32 is over. Colours: a colour element (`p:to`, `p:by` or `p:from`'s child in a `p:animClr`) with more than 8 unprefixed attributes (namespace declarations, `xmlns` and `xmlns:*`, are not attributes here) or more than 8 child transforms, or more than 16 `p:animClr` behaviours with a `p:by` for one target key and first `attrName` (lower-cased) within one sequence (the main sequence, and each interactive sequence on its own); every colour cap applies to every sequence alike; checked while reading, after the click cap. Values: the main sequence's behaviours, each counted once per target key it writes (a behaviour without its own `p:tgtEl` target writes none and counts 0, R3, whatever its effect targets; a paragraph range one per paragraph, at most 256 from `st`; any other target 1) times the number of states from its step to the last (`steps.length − k` for step `k`), more than 250,000 in all is over (a bound on what the states hold); checked after the colour caps.
- **R22 Plain slide.** Past a cap, or on XML the scanner refuses, the slide plays as a plain slide: `"steps": [[]]`, one empty state, `interactive` 0, and `approximate` one of `part-too-large`, `unreadable` (both with `transition: null`), `timing-too-large`, `too-deep`, `too-many-effects`, `too-many-clicks`, `colour-too-large`, `too-many-values` (transition still read; checked in that order), or `unexpected` (an implementation's own internal failure while reading a slide it accepted; it never appears in a golden, and a conforming implementation never produces it on the corpus or the cases).
- **R23 Scope.** The contract covers well-formed XML. The Rust parser must agree on the corpus, the cases and these rules; it need not refuse exactly the same malformed inputs.
- **R23a XML text.** An attribute value is read as XML normalises it: every literal tab, line feed and carriage return in the source becomes one space (a CR LF pair one space) before character and entity references are decoded, so `&#10;` stays a line feed. An element's text (an `attrName`) first has each literal CR LF pair and each lone CR made a line feed (XML's end-of-line handling; `&#13;` stays a carriage return), drops comments and processing instructions, drops a child element's tags but keeps that child's own text in place (as a DOM's `textContent`), takes a CDATA section's content as written, decodes references in the rest, and is trimmed of surrounding whitespace: XML's four whitespace characters only (space, tab, line feed, carriage return; a no-break space or U+FEFF stays).

### What a state holds (emphasis)

Observed in PowerPoint's saves (`emphasis`, `exits-emphasis`): every emphasis behaviour PowerPoint writes carries `fill="hold"` on its `p:cTn`, as does its effect node; transparency instead holds by never ending (its effect node has no `fill`, its behaviours `dur="indefinite"`); entrance and exit filters (`p:animEffect` with a `dur`) carry no `fill`. A held value stays until the slide is left: it survives later clicks, exits and entrances (a shape that leaves and comes back keeps its size, turn, opacity and colours).

- **R24 Held.** A behaviour's value is held after its step when its effect node's `fill` is not `remove`, neither its nor its effect node's `repeatCount` is `indefinite`, and either its own `fill` is `hold` or `freeze`, or it is a `p:set` whose dur is absent, malformed or `indefinite` (a set that never ends). Nothing else is held (an absent `fill` with a `dur` is SMIL's `remove`). Containers above the effect node are not read (PowerPoint writes `hold` on all of them).
- **R25 End progress.** A held behaviour contributes its value at the end of its effect, in progress `q` from 0 to 1 of its own simple duration. Compute the effect's span `S` (its latest finite behaviour end without the effect's own repeat, as in R10–R11, in ms, a `p:set` of dur 1 counting 0), the effect's total `S × repeatCount/1000 × (2 if autoRev)`, and the effect's progress at that total as `P(t, d, r, a, acc, dec)` with `d = S`: `s = min(t, d×(2 if a)×r/1000) / d`; with autoRev, `pos = s mod 2`, `0` when `pos` is 0 and `s > 0`, else `pos` folded back past 1 (`2 − pos`); without, `pos = s mod 1`, `1` when that is 0 and `s > 0`; then eased by `acc`/`dec` (100000ths: `a = acc/100000`, `e = dec/100000`, scaled down together if `a + e > 1`; `r = 1/(1 − a/2 − e/2)`; `q < a`: `r q²/(2a)`; `q ≤ 1 − e`: `r(q − a/2)`; else `1 − r(1 − q)²/(2e)`); a `d` of 0 is `0` with autoRev, else `1`. The behaviour's own clock is that progress times `S`, minus its delay; its `q` is `P` of that with its own dur, repeat, autoRev, accel and decel; a negative clock means it never began and contributes nothing. When `S` is 0 the effect's clock is 0 (whatever its autoRev). A set that never ends has `q = 1`. Use IEEE doubles.
- **R26 Size.** `p:animScale` (100000ths, x and y apart; each an R2a decimal, absent when it isn't): with `to`, the held factor becomes `from + (to − from) × q` (`from` as written, else the factor held so far; `to` wins over `by`, as SMIL says: PowerPoint writes a shrink as `to="50000"` beside the default `by`); else with `by`, `from × (1 + (by − 1) × q)` (two grows multiply); else unchanged. Factors are of the shape's own size, about its centre. `p:anim` on `ppt_w` `ppt_h` `ppt_x` `ppt_y` is never held (PowerPoint's presets end them at the shape's own value).
- **R27 Turn.** `p:animRot` (60000ths of a degree; R2 integers): with `to`, the turn becomes `from + (to − from) × q` (`from` as written, else the turn so far); else with `by`, turn so far (or `from`) `+ by × q`; turns add. `p:anim` on `r`, `ppt_r` or `style.rotation` whose values are all numbers (R28; degrees) sets the turn to its value at `q` (R28's interpolation). A turn is an offset from the shape's own rotation, never absolute: PowerPoint writes boomerang's `style.rotation` −90 → 0 on a shape rotated 30° exactly as on an upright one.
- **R28 Opacity, and `p:anim` values.** A value element (a `p:to`'s or a `p:tav`'s `p:val`'s first child element) is a `p:strVal`, `p:fltVal`, `p:intVal` or `p:boolVal` in the PresentationML namespace (the transitional `http://schemas.openxmlformats.org/presentationml/2006/main` or the strict `http://purl.oclc.org/ooxml/presentationml/main`), whatever its prefix; any other element (another name, as `p:clrVal`, or another namespace) is no value. A value is a number when it is a `p:fltVal` or `p:intVal` whose `val` is an R2a decimal, or a `p:strVal` (or a `from`/`to`/`by` attribute) whose text is an R2a decimal (PowerPoint writes transparency as `<p:strVal val="0.5"/>`); a `p:boolVal` never is. A `p:set` on `style.opacity` to a number, or a `p:anim` on it with a value at `q`, sets the held opacity (the later replaces; it does not multiply). A `p:anim`'s value at `q` (here, R27's rotation, and the viewer's playback all use this one definition): with a `valueType` other than `num`, or `calcmode="fmla"`, it has none. With `tav`s: each at `tm/100000` (without `tm`, spread evenly from 0 to 1: the i-th of n at i/(n−1), a single one at 0), kept in document order, never sorted; any `tav` with a non-empty `fmla`, a value that isn't a number, a time outside 0–1, or a time before the one before it means no value. Then: before the first time, the first value; past the last, the last; at a time several `tav`s share, the last of them; between two times, linear (with `calcmode="discrete"`, the earlier one's value). Without `tav`s: from `from` (absent: the value held so far, opacity 1, turn 0 when nothing is) to `to`, else to `from + by`, else to the value held so far, linear (or discrete) over `q` as two keys at 0 and 1. `cases/anim-keys.xml` pins each branch at `q` 0.5.
- **R29 Colour.** Held per target and part: `fill` (`p:animClr` on `fillcolor` or `fill.color`), `line` (`stroke.color`), `text` (`style.color`): the first of the behaviour's `attrName`s that names a part decides, compared case-insensitively. Only an animClr whose `q` is 1 changes anything. Its `to` replaces the part's colour and clears the changes on it; its `by` (an `hsl` offset: `h` in 60000ths of a degree, `s` and `l` in 100000ths, or an `rgb` offset) is appended to the part's changes, applied in order on top of the drawn or replaced colour. A `p:set` with `q` 1 whose FIRST `attrName` is exactly (case-sensitive) `fill.type` with value `solid` records `solid` (PowerPoint writes it beside every fill colour, darken, lighten, desaturate and complementary colour); exactly `fill.on` or `stroke.on` with value `true` or `false` (a `strVal` of that text, or a `boolVal`; R28's value elements only, so a `p:foo val="true"` records nothing) records `on`. Drawing: a part with `solid` is drawn in one colour; with a `to` that is the `to` colour (then its `by`s), and with only `by`s the base is the colour the part was drawn in, and for a gradient fill its first stop (the `a:gs` with the lowest `pos`): PowerPoint's film shows darken on a gradient as one solid colour, its first stop (here accent1 with satMod 130%, about 64,128,204) moved by the offset. An `hsl` offset is added in HSL (hue in degrees, wrapping; saturation and lightness 0–1, clamped): the film's darken, lighten, desaturate (fully grey) and complementary colours match to within 2 of 255. A colour is written as the element PowerPoint wrote: its local name (`type`), its unprefixed attributes, sorted by name (`attrs`; a prefixed attribute is never part of a colour, PowerPoint writes none, and is dropped), and its child transforms in order (`mods`: local name and `val`).
- **R30 Order.** Within a step, held changes apply in the order their behaviours end (effect start + R10 end; a behaviour that never ends goes last), ties in document order; across steps, in step order. Only main-sequence steps take part.
- **R31 Written form.** `scale` (target → `[x, y]`, each rounded as R18; targets at `[1, 1]` left out), `turn` (degrees reduced to `[0, 360)`, then rounded as R18; a result of 0 or 360 is left out); a held change whose result is not finite (an overflow, a NaN) is dropped and the value stays what it was, so no state holds a non-finite number, `opacity` (clamped to `[0, 1]`, rounded as R18; 1 left out), `color` (target → `fill`, `line`, `text` in that order, each with `to`, `by` (non-empty), `solid` (only `true`), `on`, in that order; empty parts and targets left out). Keys are target keys (R4) sorted in plain string order. A held change on a paragraph range is kept per paragraph, one key `spid:pN-N` for each paragraph N of the range (the first 256 from `st` at most), so where ranges overlap the change that applies later (R30) wins on the paragraphs they share; font colour by paragraph is `"7:p0-0"`, `"7:p1-1"`, … (PowerPoint's UI writes one `pRg` per paragraph at every build level, so its files never overlap; `cases/paragraph-order.xml` pins the rule).
- **R32 Split shapes.** A shape is split when any behaviour of the main sequence targets one of its paragraph ranges (`p:txEl/p:pRg`), held or not, of any kind. PowerPoint then draws its text apart from its body, and a held change on the whole shape (its key without `:bg` or a range) acts on the body alone: it is held under `spid:bg` (so a whole-shape grow after a `:bg` grow multiplies with it, a whole-shape transparency replaces the `:bg` one), and a whole-shape text colour holds nothing. PowerPoint's film of the `order` deck: a font colour on the whole box leaves the text as it was, before and after the paragraphs' own colours (slide 1, 1.875–4.875 s); a transparency on the whole box fades the fill and not the text (slide 2, `FadeBoxThenPara` at 6.25 s: fill 200,219,252, text 251,255,255), and after the paragraphs' and `:bg`'s own changes nothing more; a grow on the whole box after the box's and paragraphs' own grows takes the box to 2.25 times its height (713 px of 317, ending at y 690 at 9.875 s) and leaves the text at 1.5. The viewer plays an emphasis effect on a split shape's whole on its body too. Visibility (R5, R16, R17) and paths (R18) keep their own rules on a split shape (no film covers a path or an exit on one yet).
- **R33 Colour modifiers.** Where a parser resolves a colour itself (a held `to` with modifiers, the base of a `by`, a contrast check), its child transforms apply in order to the colour so far, in sRGB 0–1: `tint` and `shade` in linear light (each channel decoded from sRGB, `shade` multiplies by `val/100000`, `tint` is `1 − val/100000 × (1 − x)`, then encoded back; `val/100000` and each resulting channel clamped to 0–1), `lumMod`, `lumOff`, `satMod`, `satOff`, `hueMod`, `hueOff` in HSL (clamped), `alpha`, `alphaMod`, `alphaOff` on alpha; others are skipped. A transform's `val` is an optional `-` and 1–10 ASCII digits, nothing else; any other `val` skips that transform. PowerPoint's own film of flat accent1 fills with each transform (`colors.json`, `film`) matches this within 2 of 255 on every vector; tint and shade applied in sRGB were off by up to 53 (`shade` 25 %: 20,32,47 against the film's 37,66,100). `colors.json`'s `rgb` is what a conforming parser computes, to within 0.5. Except: a held `to` (R29) that is an `a:schemeClr` is the scheme colour alone, its child transforms not applied, on every part (`fill`, `line`, `text`). PowerPoint's film of the `colours` deck (movie export; the click plays 2.00–3.05 s on slide 1 and 7.05–8.05 s on slide 2) holds plain accent2 (C0504D; film 189,77,74, the film reading about 3 low) at 4.5 s for fills of accent2 with `tint` 40 %, `shade` 50 %, `lumMod` 60 % + `lumOff` 40 % and `lumMod` 75 %, and at 9.5 s for 24 pt lines and 72 pt text of accent2 with `tint` 40 %, `shade` 50 % and `lumMod` 60 % + `lumOff` 40 %; an `a:srgbClr` 00A000 with `shade` 50 % holds 0,113,0 on the fill, the line and the text alike (the linear-light shade, 0,116.35,0, `#007400`, within 4); a plain accent3 fill holds 152,183,86 (9BBB59), so the `to` is read. Other colour types in a held `to` keep their transforms (not filmed).
